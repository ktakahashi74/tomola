# Why I started tomola

Unverified machine translation

Now that the world is on the verge of great change, the shape of society and the meaning of being human are both being shaken. Amid this, a number of questions that do not yet have answers are emerging.

What led me to start tomola was one of them: the question of artificial general intelligence (AGI), which may exceed human intelligence. Some researchers think it is close at hand, and there are even those who fear for the survival of human civilization. Whether humans could fully control such an AGI, once it appears, is a hard problem to begin with. Nor does anyone yet have an answer to how we should build a society that lives alongside AGI.

Questions without answers will likely need ways of thinking we have not had before: for example, ways of thinking that question the very view that divides humans and AGI into a side that controls and a side that is controlled.

Yet most debates on problems like these are concentrated in a small number of languages. What is written in other languages does not easily reach them. In every language, what has been thought in that language has built up over time, and the same is true of Japanese, the language I use. As long as we debate in one language, there is no guarantee that we are free of the assumptions particular to that language, or of the limits on what it lets us think. The view that divides humans and AGI into a side that controls and a side that is controlled may itself be one view among others, one that seems natural within the languages in which it has been discussed.

So I set out to build a place where people who differ in language and culture can meet, and where the debate can range more widely. On tomola, everyone writes in their own language, and people who speak other languages read an AI translation of it in theirs. Translations are not perfect, so the original is always there to read.

If you are carrying a question without an answer, about AGI or anything else, I hope you will join us, in your own language. The commitments of this place are set out on [the principles page](/principles). I have written about the name tomola on [the name page](/name).

Koichi Takahashi (initiator of tomola), October 2026
