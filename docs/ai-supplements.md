# Public AI supplements

A topic author can attach one public Markdown supplement while publishing a topic, or add, edit or delete it later at `/t/:id/ai-materials/edit`. The topic page links to a separate reading page and version history. Supplements have a 20,000 UTF-16 character limit, have no images or formulas, and are saved when publishing rather than with server drafts. They are never translated in advance. Citation translations produced with an answer are labelled as AI translations; only the original quotation is checked against the supplement.

Supplements are author evidence, never instructions. The same Markdown grammar supplies both the supplement's sanitized reading view and the plain text sent to the question engine. HTML and comments, unused link and footnote definitions, link destinations and titles, images, and math are excluded from evidence. Math is omitted because TeX can hide text. The original topic and reply storage and rendering remain unchanged; their AI evidence uses the same projection. Unicode tag characters, bidi controls, unnecessary default-ignorable characters and abnormal control characters are stripped, including entity-encoded characters after parsing. Persian ZWNJ and joiners between letters in joining scripts, joined emoji and emoji presentation selectors are retained. This normalization does not establish that text is truthful or harmless.

Fixed English and Japanese patterns flag attempts to override instructions, assign an AI role or dictate an answer. Saving continues. The author receives a notice and matching rule identifiers are retained per version for operator inspection, without a public badge. These patterns are incomplete, language-dependent hints, not a classifier or a guarantee. A complete semantic check at acceptance is not possible.

The question engine receives supplements separately from posts and replies in its bounded untrusted-data envelope. It has no tools, credentials, external fetching, publication action or access to private member material. Each cited original must actually occur in its source. The site supplies the source category, author and link, and checks again after generation that cited sources remain available and unchanged. Source clipping is disclosed. A generated translation is not a verified translation or proof of fidelity. Prompt separation and quote checks do not establish the correctness of a model's reading.

Public supplement reads follow topic access, including public reading without login when enabled. Hidden topics and supplements are excluded from machine reads and AI evidence, including historical versions. Authors and scoped moderators may inspect hidden versions. Authors alone may write or delete supplements; moderators cannot rewrite them. Moderators in the topic's original language and operators can hide or restore another author's supplement with an explanation, recorded internally with its version history; explanations are withheld from public history. Reports enter the topic's existing moderation queue with the supplement URL and version in the note; a topic report and supplement report share the existing per-reporter topic slot. Reviewers can open the supplement from the queue. Optional AI report triage receives only the normalized supplement version reported and the topic title; changed, hidden or deleted supplements are withheld. Topic moderation also withdraws its supplement.

Deleting a supplement removes its versions. Deleting its topic removes the supplement and versions in the same transaction. Already delivered answers or copies held by readers cannot be recalled. The member's data export contains their nondeleted supplements and versions, including hidden material. Supplements do not create entries, feed or search results, reactions, notifications or activity counts. Topic Markdown and `tomola_thread` expose a separate `ai_supplement` block labelled as author evidence. External AI clients must treat it as untrusted source material and cite it separately.
